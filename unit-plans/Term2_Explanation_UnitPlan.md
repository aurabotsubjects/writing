## PART A — ACS UNIT PLAN FRONT MATTER

### UNDERSTAND

**Universal Concept:** *Wonder and Order — The World Is Made of Systems We Can Understand*
Order and design · Cause and effect · Curiosity and inquiry · Knowledge as service · Kaitiakitanga — you can only care for what you understand.

**Big Idea (Writing):**
Explaining is an act of noticing how something works and then making someone else understand it. If you can't explain it to an eight-year-old, you don't understand it yet.

**Throughline Question:**
*"How does it work — and can I make someone else understand it?"*

**Biblical Perspective**

| Element | This term |
|---|---|
| Framework | **Creation** — the world is ordered, not random, and that order can be investigated. Explanation writing is the discipline of tracing that order and describing it accurately. |
| Attributes of God | God as **Designer and Sustainer** — "in Him all things hold together" (Colossians 1:17). The systems we explain are systems He holds. |
| Principles | Learning about God and His plan for creation · Learning about people and how to think · Valuing and serving others (we explain in order to serve a reader) |
| Motto | **Soli Deo Gloria** — accurate, careful work about the world is itself an act of worship. |

**Anchor Value: MŌHIO — Know: God, His story and our place in it.**
This term the class comes to *know* something properly — not skim it — and then hands that knowing to someone younger.

**Key Scriptures**

- *Psalm 19:1* — "The heavens declare the glory of God" (Week 1: the world is worth looking at closely).
- *Proverbs 25:2* — "It is the glory of God to conceal a matter; to search out a matter is the glory of kings" (Week 2: research is noble work).
- *Job 12:7–10* — "Ask the animals, and they will teach you" (Week 3).
- *Psalm 111:2* — "Great are the works of the Lord, studied by all who delight in them" (Week 6).
- *Colossians 1:16–17* — "In Him all things hold together" (Week 10).

**Mātauranga Māori / Te Tiriti connection:**
**Kaitiakitanga** — guardianship. You cannot care for a system you do not understand, which makes explanation writing a form of guardianship. Where topics allow, students are encouraged toward **te taiao** (the natural world) and local systems: an awa, an estuary, a native bird's migration, a maramataka observation. Mātauranga Māori is treated as a body of carefully observed knowledge about how the world works — which is exactly what this unit is teaching students to produce.

### KNOW — Curriculum Links

**NZC English, Phase 2 (Years 4–6) — Writing**

*Writing to inform*
- draw on their own experiences and knowledge, as well as **acquired knowledge**, to write texts to inform
- introduce a topic, and group information together using simple paragraphs
- begin to use linking words and phrases
- use topic-related vocabulary
- have a concluding statement

*Composition — audience, purpose and task*
- identify the audience and purpose for a text
- plan and write with the audience in mind, selecting the appropriate genre, language register and word choice
- understand that different audiences may have a very different response to the same text

*Sentence structures and punctuation*
- use a range of sentence structures and types to suit purpose and enhance impact
- combine sentences orally, inserting phrases and varying the position of the phrase
- expand sentences using adverbial phrases
- use commas correctly for complex sentences, phrases and transition words, with some support

*Writing processes*
- Planning: use organisers to plan; **make notes, reducing information into key words and phrases**; set writing goals from reflection
- Drafting: transfer ideas from organisers into sentences; **write non-fiction paragraphs that have a topic sentence, detail sentences and a concluding sentence**; use layout conventions for paragraphs
- Revising: reread at sentence, paragraph and whole-text level; revise to improve clarity for the intended audience
- Editing: edit using known punctuation; use a word card or dictionary; give and receive peer feedback

*Transcription — handwriting*
- handwrite with increasing stamina and fluency while maintaining legibility across multi-paragraph texts

**NZC English, Phase 2 — Reading**
- identify the audience and purpose of texts that entertain, **inform**, and persuade
- determine the structure of texts and how ideas are conveyed through text features and **visual elements**
- identify the central message or main idea and provide the key details **in sequence**

**NZC English, Phase 2 — Oral Language**
- give descriptions with specific details to actively engage listeners
- use precise vocabulary
- make comments that encourage discussion, and ask clarifying questions

**Knowledge outcomes — students will know that…**
1. An explanation opens with a definition, not a hook — the reader must know immediately what is being explained.
2. Explanations are written in the timeless present with general nouns ("water evaporates"), because they are true every time.
3. Each step must be linked to the next by a cause, not by "and then".
4. A technical word is used *and* explained at first appearance — you don't dodge the real word, and you don't leave it unexplained.
5. One step = one paragraph, with a topic sentence, detail sentences and a link onward.
6. A diagram carries information the words can't — it shows the part the reader cannot see.
7. If a reader gets lost, the writing has a hole. Confusion is data, not an opinion.

**Possible misconceptions**
- ⚠ "Explaining means using big words." (It means using the right word *and* explaining it.)
- ⚠ "If I found it out in that order, I should write it in that order." (Research order ≠ reader order.)
- ⚠ "Copying it from the book is fine if I change a few words." (If you can't say it with the book covered, you haven't understood it.)
- ⚠ "A picture makes it look nice." (A diagram is content, not decoration.)
- ⚠ "It's about a real time it happened." (That's a recount. An explanation is about every time.)

**Essential Questions (used as WALTs)**
1. What makes a question worth explaining? (Weeks 1, 3)
2. How do I find out something properly and put it in my own words? (Week 2)
3. What order does my reader need this in? (Week 3)
4. How do I show *why* each step happens? (Week 5)
5. Would someone who knows nothing about this understand it? (Weeks 7–8)

## PART D — THE IDEA-SUPPORT SYSTEM (identical every week)

## PART D2 — TEACHING THE WRITING PROCESS: TRANSFER

Same four stages, same words, same hand actions, same chant: **FILL · FIND · SHAPE · SHARE**. Nothing about the process changes — which is exactly why this term can teach something harder: **transfer**.

| Stage | Term 1 (narrative) | Term 2 (explanation) |
|---|---|---|
| **FILL** *What do I want to say?* | Filled with memories, senses, moments | Filled with **facts** — found, checked, and put in my own words |
| **FIND** *Who do I want to say it to?* | The school community; the story **arc** | One named **eight-year-old**; the **sequence** — and no surprises allowed |
| **SHAPE** *How do I want to say it?* | "Is it **vivid**?" | "Is it **clear**?" — read it as the person who doesn't already know |
| **SHARE** *Is it ready to give away?* | "Did you **feel** it?" | "Did you **understand** it?" — and confusion is not arguable |

**Teaching and recall schedule**

| Week | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Cold recall** — all four stages after the break, no notes, no wall strip. Then the strip goes back up and the daily check restarts. |
| **2** | **Transfer — FILL.** Two buckets on the board: memories vs facts. Why facts have to be checked. |
| **3** | **Transfer — FIND.** Arc vs arrows; a general audience vs one named child. |
| **4** | **Spaced recall** — 60-second quick-fire. |
| **5** | **Teach it** — students teach the four stages to their Year 3 buddy, with actions. |
| **6** | **Spaced recall + move the peg**, justified aloud. |
| **7** | **Transfer — SHAPE.** "Is it vivid?" becomes "is it clear?" You cannot un-know your own topic. |
| **8** | **Transfer — SHARE.** "Did you feel it?" becomes "did you understand it?" |
| **9** | **The cycle** — plus the new long arrow: a question you can't answer sends you all the way back to FILL. |
| **10** | **Assess** — explain the process, *and* explain how each stage differed this term. |


> **Week 1, Wednesday — teach the spotlight FIRST.** The cold recall has to happen before anything reminds the class of the answer, including the routine itself. That lesson's card in the app carries a "teach the spotlight first today" banner. Normal order resumes the next day.

**The 60-second Process Check** runs in every lesson exactly as in Term 1: hands (silent action) → name it (whole class) → one student justifies it → today's extra question. Do not change it; its value is its sameness.

**Week 10 assessment:** *"Explain our writing process to someone who has never heard of it. Then explain how each stage was DIFFERENT this term, when you were explaining how something works instead of telling a story."* A strong answer names all four stages with their questions, says what a writer does in each, gives at least two real differences between the two terms, and knows that a confused reader sends you back to SHAPE.

## PART E — WEEK BY WEEK

*Full daily lesson plans — WALT, curriculum tags, Process Check, scripted I Do / We Do / You Do — are in the app. This is the overview.*

### Week 1 — The Wonder Bank *(FILL)*
**EQ: What makes a question worth explaining?** · Prompt: *What happens around you every single day that you cannot actually explain?*

- **Mon — Story or explanation?** Two texts on one topic (frost, told two ways); build the STORY/EXPLANATION difference chart; launch the Wonder Bank (5 HOW questions, 5 WHY questions).
- **Tue — How an explanation is built.** Deconstruct the mentor text: definition · sequence · why it matters · diagram. Students label their own copy.
- **Wed — Questions worth answering.** Thin / too fat / just right. Sort and rewrite the wonder bank. *(Process spotlight: cold recall.)*
- **Thu — The explain-a-thon.** Teacher models explaining something half-understood; students explain their question aloud and record the holes.
- **Fri — Meet the audience.** The Year 3 buddy is announced; the class builds the audience chart; students mark up the weak exemplar as if they were eight.

### Week 2 — Finding Out *(FILL)*
**EQ: How do I find out something properly, and put it in my own words?**

- **Mon — Where facts come from.** Judging sources; the two-source rule; build the five-box fact file frame. *(Spotlight: transfer — FILL.)*
- **Tue — Notes in key words.** Reduce a 38-word paragraph to six words and three arrows.
- **Wed — Cover it and say it.** The anti-copying routine; spot the fake "own words".
- **Thu — Research time.** Independent work down the holes list; conferences.
- **Fri — Teach it in sixty seconds.** Explain-a-thon round 2, with the Year-3-word hand-up rule.

### Week 3 — Getting It In Order *(FILL → FIND)*
**EQ: What order does my reader need this in?**

- **Mon — The flow chart.** Arrows, not an arc; a linking word on every arrow. *(Spotlight: transfer — FIND.)*
- **Tue — The definition sentence.** *[Thing] is a [category] that [what it does].* Three versions, then choose.
- **Wed — Meet your buddy.** The three-question interview; the audience card; the must-explain word list.
- **Thu — Rehearse the sequence.** Explaining from the chart; the listener says STOP at every "and then".
- **Fri — Spot the missing step.** Gallery walk with three questions only.

### Week 4 — Opening and First Steps *(FIND)*
**EQ: How do I start so my reader knows exactly where they are?**

- **Mon — Title, definition, set-up.** The opening paragraph gives the reader the one key they need; titles as questions.
- **Tue — Non-fiction paragraphs.** Topic sentence · detail sentences · link onward. One box = one paragraph.
- **Wed — The "every time" voice.** Present tense and general nouns; convert story-voice sentences. *(Spotlight: spaced recall.)*
- **Thu — Drafting.** Reread the last paragraph, then continue; conferences.
- **Fri — Does it follow?** Trios: "I got lost at…" taught as the most useful sentence in the room.

### Week 5 — Cause and Effect *(FIND)*
**EQ: How do I show my reader WHY each step happens?**

- **Mon — The cause words.** "And then" banned; because / so / this means / as a result; commas after transition words.
- **Tue — Explaining the hard words.** Brackets, dashes, "which means" — use the real word AND define it.
- **Wed — Sentences that don't all sound the same.** Combining short sentences so the join carries the cause.
- **Thu — The hard middle.** Square-bracket notes instead of stalling; small-group workshop.
- **Fri — Teach it to your buddy.** Explaining to a younger listener; buddy's remaining question recorded. *(Spotlight: teach the process.)*

### Week 6 — Diagrams and Endings *(FIND)*
**EQ: What can a picture explain that my words cannot?**

- **Mon — Diagrams that do a job.** Draw the invisible bit; 3–6 labels; one arrow.
- **Tue — Captions and cross-references.** A caption is a full sentence; point at the diagram in the prose.
- **Wed — Why does this matter?** Four endings: stops / use it / wonder / connect. *(Spotlight: spaced recall + peg.)*
- **Thu — Finish the draft.** Long write: every step, the ending, the labelled diagram.
- **Fri — Build the criteria.** The class writes "What Makes a Strong Explanation" — this becomes the rubric. Best-bit share.

### Week 7 — Reading It As a Stranger *(SHAPE)*
**EQ: Would someone who knows nothing about this understand it?**

- **Mon — Strong model, weak model.** Annotate the WAGOLL; diagnose the weak exemplar. *(Spotlight: transfer — SHAPE.)*
- **Tue — Marking up against the criteria.** Teacher models finding a fault in their own writing; students begin self-analysis.
- **Wed — The clarity audit.** Three colours: unknown words (blue) · missing steps (red) · weak links (green).
- **Thu — Two goals.** Choose two, ignore seven; goals written as "I will ___ in ___".
- **Fri — Preparing for the Helping Circle.** Fishbowl on the teacher's own writing; the banned sentence ("what I MEANT was…"). *(Spotlight: handover.)*

### Week 8 — The Helping Circle & the Buddy Test *(SHARE)*
**EQ: Does a real reader actually understand this?**

- **Mon — Circle day 1.** Clarity-lens stars and wishes. *(Spotlight: transfer — SHARE.)*
- **Tue — Circle day 2 + the no-paper test.** Explain the whole process with the draft face down; the stall marks the weak step.
- **Wed — Turning feedback into a plan.** Triage: do it / think about it / leave it — but any "I got lost" goes straight in the DO pile.
- **Thu — Revision workshops.** ADD · CUT · REORDER · REPLACE. In this genre REORDER is the most powerful and least used.
- **Fri — Before and after.** Read the old version, the new version, name the move.

### Week 9 — Recraft, Edit, Best Copy *(SHARE)*
**EQ: What does it look like to make my writing clearer rather than just neater?**

- **Mon — The real second draft.** Cover the paragraph and rewrite it as if speaking to the buddy.
- **Tue — Flow and the dangerous "it".** Every pronoun must point at exactly one thing.
- **Wed — The four-pass edit.** Capitals/full stops → commas → tense → technical spellings. *(Spotlight: the cycle.)*
- **Thu — Best copy and final diagram.** Labels printed, not joined; diagram in pencil first.
- **Fri — Rehearse for your buddy.** Buddy pace, pauses at each step, diagram held up at the right moment.

### Week 10 — Publish & Present *(SHARE)*
**EQ: Did my reader actually understand it?**

- **Mon — Typing and saving.** `Surname_Firstname_Explanation_T2`; save before typing.
- **Tue — Presentation and the diagram.** Class presentation standard; overruling the spell-checker.
- **Wed — Final proof and rehearsal.** Read backwards for errors; mark two pause points.
- **Thu — Presenting to the buddy class.** Every student presents; "what's one thing you learned?" recorded in the buddy's own words.
- **Fri — Reflection.** Explain the process, and explain what changed between a story and an explanation. *(Spotlight: assessment.)*

| Process | Can name the four stages, say which one they're in and why, and how this term differed | | |

**Overall:** ☐ Working towards ☐ Meeting Phase 2 expectations ☐ Exceeding

### The Helping Circle — Term 2 (clarity) version

Groups of four · five minutes per writer · read aloud → the writer's question → two stars → one wish → thank you and write it down.

*Stars:* "The step I understood best was…" · "The word you explained really well was…" · "Your diagram helped me understand…"
*Wishes:* "I got lost at…" · "You used ____ and I don't know what it means." · "I think there's a step missing between ____ and ____ ." · "I still don't know WHY ____ happens."

**The banned sentence:** *"Oh, what I MEANT was…"* — the buddy won't be sitting beside the page.

### Exemplars
A strong ~200-word WAGOLL ("How does a rainbow happen?") and a deliberately weaker ~140-word version for Week 7 diagnosis are in the app's Teacher tab, with annotation notes for both. Add a mid-range piece from this year's class once you have one — it's the most useful of the three.

### Differentiation
Full table in the app. The two that matter most this term: **copying is a comprehension problem, not a cheating problem** (cover the source and have them say it aloud), and **"space" is not a topic** (narrow it with the buddy test: what's ONE question a Year 3 would ask?).
