# TERM 3 — WRITING FOCUS UNIT PLAN
## Persuasive Writing & Speeches: *"Words That Change Things"*
### Years 5–6 (Ages 9–12) · 10 weeks · ~25 min per lesson, 5 days per week

Every lesson exists in full lesson-plan form in the **Writing Focus** app (WALT, NZC link and tags, the daily Process Check, scripted I Do / We Do / You Do). This document is the planning overview.

> **Two assumptions you can change.** (1) Every student writes **and delivers** a 2-minute speech in Week 10 — the written piece and the speech are the *same argument in two forms*, not two pieces of work. (2) There's no fixed external competition date, so speeches land in Week 10. If your school runs a speech competition earlier in the term, move the Week 8–10 speech strand forward and let the written publication follow the speech instead of preceding it.

## PART A — ACS UNIT PLAN FRONT MATTER

### UNDERSTAND

**Universal Concept:** *Voice and Service — Words Are One of the Ways We Serve People*
Justice and fairness · Evidence and truth · Listening to the other side · Courage · Using a gift on someone else's behalf.

**Big Idea (Writing):**
An opinion is where a writer starts, not what they hand in. Persuasion is what happens when you find out whether you're right, understand who you're asking, and ask them well.

**Throughline Question:**
*"What needs to change — and can my words move someone to change it?"*

**Biblical Perspective**

| Element | This term |
|---|---|
| Framework | **Redemption and Restoration** — naming what is broken and asking for it to be put right is the shape of prophetic speech throughout Scripture. |
| Attributes of God | God as **Just** and as **Advocate** — He hears the case of those with no standing to make one. |
| Principles | Valuing and serving others · Learning about people and how to think · Developing faith and character |
| Motto | **Soli Deo Gloria** — we argue honestly, because the truth is not ours to bend. |

**Anchor Value: MANAAKI — Serve faithfully with our gifts and talents.**
Words are the gift this term. Every student is pushed past "what annoys me" toward "who could this serve?" — and the strongest arguments in the class are usually the ones made on someone else's behalf.

**Key Scriptures**

- *Proverbs 31:8–9* — "Speak up for those who cannot speak for themselves" (Week 1).
- *Ephesians 4:29* — words that build others up (Week 2: the rules of engagement).
- *Micah 6:8* — act justly, love mercy, walk humbly (Week 3: choosing an issue worth the effort).
- *Proverbs 15:1* — "A gentle answer turns away wrath" (Week 6: how we treat the other side).
- *Colossians 4:6* — "full of grace, seasoned with salt" (Week 10: how we speak when we want to win).

**Mātauranga Māori / Te Tiriti connection:**
**Whaikōrero** and the marae as a place where argument is made *formally, respectfully and in front of people*. Students meet the idea that in te ao Māori a case is not won by volume — it is won by standing, by whakapapa of reasoning, and by respect for the person you disagree with. Also **mana** and **manaakitanga**: an argument that diminishes the person you are addressing costs you your own mana. Where topics allow, students are encouraged toward local kaupapa — an awa, a reserve, a road crossing, a school practice — where a real decision-maker exists.

### KNOW — Curriculum Links

**NZC English, Phase 2 (Years 4–6) — Writing**

*Writing to persuade*
- state a clear position and support it with reasons and evidence
- address the audience directly and anticipate the counter-argument
- end with a call to action

*Composition — audience, purpose and task*
- identify the audience and purpose for a text
- plan and write with the audience in mind, selecting the appropriate genre, language register and word choice
- understand that different audiences may have a very different response to the same text

*Writing processes — drafting*
- write paragraphs with a topic sentence, detail sentences and a concluding sentence (taught here as **PEEL**: Point, Evidence, Explain, Link)
- transfer ideas from planning organisers into sentences
- use layout conventions to indicate paragraphs

*Sentence structures and punctuation*
- use a range of sentence structures and types to suit purpose and enhance impact
- expand sentences using adverbial phrases; combine sentences, varying phrase position
- use commas correctly for complex sentences, phrases and **transition words**, with some support

*Writing craft*
- vocabulary chosen deliberately for effect, including **connotation** and precision
- figurative language used purposefully, not decoratively

*Writing processes — revising and editing*
- reread at sentence, paragraph and whole-text level
- revise to improve clarity and impact for the intended audience
- edit using known punctuation; give and receive peer feedback

*Transcription*
- handwrite with increasing stamina and fluency, maintaining legibility across multi-paragraph texts

**NZC English, Phase 2 — Oral Language** *(carrying unusual weight this term)*
- communicate in complete sentences, incorporating a range of conjunctions and connectives, and **varying sentence structure and length to create effect and support meaning**
- give descriptions with specific details to **actively engage listeners**
- use precise vocabulary
- make comments that encourage discussion, ask clarifying questions, and **consider the impact of their words on others**

**NZC English, Phase 2 — Reading**
- identify the audience and purpose of texts that entertain, inform and **persuade**
- respond to texts by sharing opinions and personal thoughts and feelings
- **acknowledge others' responses and respond respectfully to differences**

**Key competency — Thinking (named explicitly in the ACS template):**
- **"Argumentation: making a claim and supporting it with evidence"** — the central skill of the whole unit
- Asking curious or critical questions; cause-and-effect thinking; metacognition

**Knowledge outcomes — students will know that…**
1. An opinion, a reason and evidence are three different things, and only the third one ends an argument.
2. "Everyone" is not an audience. A persuasive text is aimed at the one person who can say yes.
3. Reasons are ordered deliberately: easiest to agree with first, strongest last.
4. A PEEL paragraph does four jobs, and the **Explain** is where the persuading actually happens.
5. Naming the other side's best argument fairly makes you *more* credible, not less.
6. A call to action must be specific, small and inside the audience's power to grant.
7. You can be right and still lose, if you sound like you're attacking the person who has to decide.
8. A listener cannot reread you — so a speech needs shorter sentences, louder signposts and real pauses.

**Possible misconceptions**
- ⚠ "Persuasive writing means saying it more strongly." (It means proving it. Volume is not evidence.)
- ⚠ "I should ignore the other side so people don't think of it." (They'll think of it anyway, and then you look like you hadn't.)
- ⚠ "Emotive words are the technique." (One notch up is powerful; three is exaggeration, and exaggeration costs you trust.)
- ⚠ "The bigger the ask, the better." (The smaller the ask, the likelier the yes — and a small yes is where change starts.)
- ⚠ "Changing my mind means I lost." (It means the evidence worked. That's the point of gathering it.)
- ⚠ "A speech is just my essay read out." (It's the same argument re-registered for the ear: about a third shorter.)

**Essential Questions (used as WALTs)**
1. What is worth arguing about? (Week 1)
2. What's the difference between an opinion and an argument? (Week 2)
3. Who am I trying to move, and what will move them? (Week 3)
4. How do I make my evidence mean something to my audience? (Week 5)
5. Would this convince someone who doesn't already agree with me? (Week 7)
6. Can my words move someone? (Week 10)

## PART C — THE WEEKLY RHYTHM

| Day | Role | Balance |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | **Launch** — new focus through a model or demonstration | I Do–heavy |
| **Tuesday** | **Build together** — the class constructs an example jointly | We Do–heavy |
| **Wednesday** | **Stretch** — a harder application of the same skill | We Do → You Do |
| **Thursday** | **Write** — students apply it to their own argument | You Do–heavy |
| **Friday** | **Share and reflect** — trios, circles, and **standing to read** | Student voice |

**Every 25-minute lesson:** Warm-Up Bridge (2) → Process Check (1) → I Do (5–8) → We Do (5–8) → You Do (7–10) → Close (1–2).

**The standing habit:** from Week 4, every Friday share is done standing, from a marked speaking spot in the room. By Week 10 standing up is ordinary. This is the single most important piece of anxiety management in the unit — do not skip it.

Term 3's warm-up skills are unusually well matched: **modal verbs** (W3), **connectives for persuasive writing** and **PEEL** (W4), **connotation** (W5), **formal vs informal register** (W6), **rhetorical questions** (W7), **parenthesis** (W8). Each is named in that day's bridge.

## PART D2 — TEACHING THE WRITING PROCESS: THE THIRD GENRE

Same four stages, third term: **FILL · FIND · SHAPE · SHARE**. One genuinely new idea this term — **the piece doesn't finish on the page**.

| Stage | Term 1 (narrative) | Term 2 (explanation) | Term 3 (persuasive) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FILL** | Memories and senses | Facts, checked | Reasons, evidence — **and the other side's evidence** |
| **FIND** | The school community; the story **arc** | One eight-year-old; the **sequence** | The person who can **say yes**; the **case** |
| **SHAPE** | "Is it **vivid**?" | "Is it **clear**?" | "Would it **convince** someone who disagrees?" |
| **SHARE** | "Did you **feel** it?" | "Did you **understand** it?" | "**Were you convinced**?" — and it ends on a stage |

**Teaching and recall schedule**

| Week | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Cold recall** (third time, no warning) — then announce the Week 10 speech, so ten weeks of standing practice has a purpose. |
| **2** | **Transfer — FILL.** Two columns: my evidence / their evidence. Changing your mind is a success, not a failure. |
| **3** | **Transfer — FIND.** "EVERYONE" written on the board and crossed out. |
| **4** | **Spaced recall** — quick-fire, no notes. |
| **5** | **Three genres, one process** — each stage explained three ways; the class generates the four constants. |
| **6** | **Spaced recall + peg**, plus the three SHAPE questions on the wall: vivid → clear → convincing. |
| **7** | **Transfer — SHAPE.** "You can be right and still lose." |
| **8** | **Transfer — SHARE.** Delivered from the speaking spot. The irony rule: you may not argue with feedback on your persuasive writing. |
| **9** | **The cycle** + the rehearsal loop: every stumble out loud is an edit. |
| **10** | **Assess** — three genres, one process, and what never changed. |


> **Week 1, Wednesday — teach the spotlight FIRST.** The cold recall has to happen before anything reminds the class of the answer, including the routine itself. That lesson's card in the app carries a "teach the spotlight first today" banner. Normal order resumes the next day.

**The four constants** (generated by the class in Week 5, assessed in Week 10): you always fill before you draft · you always write to a particular someone · you always read it as the person who doesn't already agree · you always test it on a real human before you publish.

## PART E — WEEK BY WEEK

*Full daily lesson plans are in the app. This is the overview.*

### Week 1 — What Needs to Change? *(FILL)*
- **Mon — Persuasion is everywhere.** Three texts, one question: what does this want me to DO? Build the "How Persuasion Works" chart; launch the Issue Bank.
- **Tue — How a speech works.** Watch 60–90 seconds of a real speech twice; build the SPOKEN vs WRITTEN chart.
- **Wed — Complaint or argument?** Same feeling, two versions; upgrade complaints into arguments with a number, a name and an ask. *(Spotlight: cold recall + announce the speech.)*
- **Thu — The 30-second case.** Standing, in pairs. Listeners count "I think" and ask "how do you know?"
- **Fri — Whose voice, and for whom?** Proverbs 31:8–9; who does this issue serve? Shortlist to three.

### Week 2 — Building a Case *(FILL)*
- **Mon — Opinion, reason, evidence.** The three-layer test; start the case file. *(Spotlight: transfer — FILL.)*
- **Tue — Four kinds of evidence.** Number · example · expert · story. Plan the evidence hunt.
- **Wed — The other side.** Steelman the opposition; the objection page.
- **Thu — Evidence hunt.** Count it yourself: tally sheets, interviews, the school's own documents.
- **Fri — The case test.** Trios: "How do you know?" · "What would someone who disagrees say?" · "What exactly are you asking for?"

### Week 3 — Who Can Say Yes? *(FILL → FIND)*
- **Mon — The yes-test.** Three audiences, three versions; choose and commit to the issue. *(Spotlight: transfer — FIND.)*
- **Tue — The claim sentence.** Modal verbs: could / should / must / ought to.
- **Wed — Building the plan.** Four boxes; reasons ordered with the strongest last.
- **Thu — Rehearse it on a real "no".** A partner plays the decision-maker and refuses, with a reason.
- **Fri — Plan gallery.** Would you say yes? Which reason is weakest? Is the ask doable?

### Week 4 — Opening and Claim *(FIND)*
- **Mon — Four ways to open.** Statistic · question · story · direct address.
- **Tue — Signposting.** "There are three reasons…"; the connective bank sorted into starting / adding / turning / landing.
- **Wed — PEEL.** Point, Evidence, Explain, Link — and why the Explain is where persuading happens. *(Spotlight: spaced recall.)*
- **Thu — Drafting.** Audience card first, then the pen.
- **Fri — Openings, standing up.** The standing habit begins: stand, breathe, look up.

### Week 5 — Reasons That Land *(FIND)*
- **Mon — Words that carry weight.** Connotation: cold / warm / too hot. One notch, not three.
- **Tue — Putting evidence into sentences.** Dumped vs woven; attribution ("our duty teacher logged…").
- **Wed — So what?** The three-rung ladder from fact to the thing the audience cares about.
- **Thu — Reasons two and three.** Sustained drafting; small-group workshop on the Explain.
- **Fri — Were you convinced?** Two feedback sentences only. *(Spotlight: three genres, one process.)*

### Week 6 — The Other Side and the Ask *(FIND)*
- **Mon — Concede and turn.** Ignore / sneer / concede-and-turn — and why the third one strengthens the argument.
- **Tue — The ask.** Too big / too vague / a plea / the real ask. Shrink it until it can be granted this term.
- **Wed — Rhythm and the rule of three.** Three, then a short sentence. *(Spotlight: recall + peg; the three SHAPE questions.)*
- **Thu — Finish the draft.** Long write: reasons, objection, ask.
- **Fri — Build the criteria.** The class writes "What Makes a Strong Argument". Best-line share, standing.

### Week 7 — Would It Convince Me? *(SHAPE)*
- **Mon — Strong model, weak model.** Annotate the WAGOLL; diagnose the weak one. *(Spotlight: transfer — SHAPE.)*
- **Tue — Marking up against the criteria.** Teacher finds the hole in their own case first.
- **Wed — The credibility audit.** Blue: unproved claims · Red: exaggeration · Green: attacks on the audience.
- **Thu — Two goals.** Including permission to *cut* a weak reason.
- **Fri — Preparing for the Helping Circle.** Fishbowl on the teacher; the irony rule. *(Spotlight: handover.)*

### Week 8 — The Helping Circle & From Page to Stage *(SHARE)*
- **Mon — Circle day 1.** Standing to read. "I started doubting you when…" *(Spotlight: transfer — SHARE.)*
- **Tue — Circle day 2 + triage.** Any "I doubted you" goes straight into the DO pile.
- **Wed — Revision workshops.** ADD · CUT · REORDER · REPLACE, with CUT emphasised.
- **Thu — From page to stage.** Cut a third; signpost out loud; mark the pauses; time it to 2 minutes.
- **Fri — First run-through.** Trios, standing, timed; mark stumbles, don't fix mid-flow.

### Week 9 — Recraft, Edit, Rehearse *(SHARE)*
- **Mon — The real second draft.** Cover it and rewrite for the person deciding.
- **Tue — The four-pass edit.** Capitals → commas after connectives → apostrophes → **names**.
- **Wed — Best copy.** Addressed, dated, signed — a letter that could actually be delivered. *(Spotlight: the cycle + the rehearsal loop.)*
- **Thu — Cue cards and the speaking voice.** Four cards, five words each; voice, pace, pause, eyes.
- **Fri — Rehearsal with feedback.** Two delivery stars and one pause suggestion. Never a comment on personality.

### Week 10 — Publish & Deliver *(SHARE)*
- **Mon — Typing and sending.** `Surname_Firstname_Persuasive_T3`. And the real question: is yours good enough to actually send?
- **Tue — Final proof.** Read backwards; draw for speaking order.
- **Wed — Dress rehearsal.** Half the class to the other half, full conditions, timed.
- **Thu — Speech day.** Every student delivers. Teacher goes first. Consider inviting the audience the speeches are addressed to.
- **Fri — Reflection.** Three genres, one process, and what never changed. *(Spotlight: assessment.)*

### The Helping Circle — Term 3 (persuasion) version

Groups of four · five minutes per writer · **the writer stands to read**, because this is heading for a stage.

*Stars:* "The reason that convinced me most was…" · "Your evidence for ____ was strong because…" · "You changed my mind about…"
*Wishes:* "I started doubting you when…" · "You said ____ but didn't prove it." · "Someone who disagrees would say ____ ." · "Your call to action isn't something I could actually do."

**The irony rule:** you may not argue with feedback on your persuasive writing. If you have to convince someone out loud that your argument was convincing, the writing didn't do it.

### Exemplars
A strong ~230-word WAGOLL ("Give us back the bottom field" — a letter to a named principal) and a deliberately weaker ~150-word version are in the app's Teacher tab, with annotation notes for both. The weak one is built around the three failures worth teaching: no evidence, attacking the audience, and calling the counter-argument stupid.

### Differentiation
Full table in the app. The three that matter most this term:
- **Only opinions, no evidence** → back to FILL for one lesson with one job: find one number, one example or one quotation.
- **Writes an attack rather than an argument** → "Would the person you're writing to want to help you after reading this?" It's a strategy question, not a manners lecture.
- **Terrified of speaking** → scale the audience, not the task: one partner → a group of four → the class from your seat → from the front. Every step counts as delivered. Cue cards plus a memorised opening line remove most of the fear.
